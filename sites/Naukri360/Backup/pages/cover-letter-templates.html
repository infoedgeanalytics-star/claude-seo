Types of Cover Letter Templates

The selection of the appropriate cover letter template can be the deciding factor in a job application's success. Depending on the circumstances, the strategy may vary, from applying for a job posting to directly contacting a company or demonstrating creativity in a competitive industry.
  1. Application Cover Letter: This should be used when seeking employment at a certain facility; it will summarize your skills and motivations related to that role.
  2. Prospecting Cover Letter: Designed for employers without any job vacancies, this letter focuses on the advantages the candidate can provide.
  3. Networking Cover Letter: Used to contact friends or acquaintances to ask for job recommendations; casual and relationship-building.
  4. Industry-Specific Templates: Created for each field (i.e., Finance, IT, Health Care, Marketing, Education), with wording specific to each recognition.
  5. Experience-Level Templates: Executive (management and influence), Mid-level (accomplishments and development), and Entry-level (education and transferable abilities).
  6. Style-Based Templates: Modern (simple designs), Creative (bold designs for creative industries), Traditional (classic designs for conservative industries).
  7. ATS-Friendly Templates: Designed to function with applicant tracking systems, these templates have straightforward designs, make use of keywords, and don't have complicated layouts or graphics.

How to Write a Winning Cover Letter (Step-by-Step)

Use this easy formula when personalizing your cover letter template:
  • Step 1: Personalize Your Introduction: Discuss the position title and your reason for applying.
  • Step 2: Highlight Relevant Achievements: Emphasize measurable achievements.
  • Step 3: Show Cultural Fit: Illustrate how you fit in the company’s values.
  • Step 4: End with Confidence: Finish with a strong call to action.

What is the Correct Cover Letter Format?

A professional cover letter form generally contains:
  • Header (Your contact information): Add your name, phone number, email address, and professional profile link.
  • Employer: Include the name of the hiring manager, company name, and company address.
  • Professional greetings: The professional greeting should be formal, like addressing the hiring manager as dear or using the name of the recruiter.
  • Introduction paragraph: Be brief about yourself and state the job position you are seeking.
  • Body paragraphs (skills and achievements): List your skills, experience, and the relevant achievements in the position.
  • Call-to-action, closing sentence: show interest in the position and inquire about a chance of an interview.
  • Professional signature: Complete politely with an ending like Sincerely, and your full name.
